# Developed Markets Equity Scorecard

A relative-performance scorecard for five developed equity markets — **US, Eurozone, UK, Switzerland and Japan** — built to rank them over a three-to-six-month horizon.

The ranking in this package is **as of 30 April 2026**, the last month in which all five markets report in the supplied dataset. It is the latest ranking the data supports, not a reading of current market conditions.

## Method in brief

| Pillar | Indicator | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Macro | Financial-conditions impulse, 6 months | Predictive (provisional) |
| Micro | Earnings-revision persistence, 12 months (share of positive monthly changes in forward EPS) | Predictive (provisional) |
| Valuation | Forward earnings yield minus 2-year government yield, against own history | Diagnostic |
| Technical | Signed trend quality, 12 months, on reconstructed prices | Diagnostic |

Each indicator is standardised across the five markets each month. The **four-pillar score** is the equal-weight average of all four; the **predictive score** averages only the two pillars with out-of-sample evidence. Valuation and Technical stay on the scorecard with their scores and contributions but carry no predictive weight. A score is acted on one month after it is formed.

## Data constraints

- The panel ends 30 April 2026.
- The supplied price workbook contains valuation ratios, not index levels. Prices are reconstructed as forward P/E × forward EPS; the US reconstruction matches a published index to 0.04 percentage points, and the other four cannot be validated against one.
- Returns are price returns in US dollars. No dividend data is supplied.
- The point-in-time status of the consensus earnings series cannot be verified.
- The US earnings field differs from the other four markets and is smoother.
- Switzerland uses the euro-area financial-conditions and credit series.
- The volatility column in the UK workbook is not an equity volatility index and is excluded.
- No external data is used anywhere.

## Reading the results

No pillar is statistically significant once the length of the research process is accounted for, and the examined window (July 2017 onward) has been looked at repeatedly and is not untouched holdout data. The results describe an improved and more stable historical relationship that justifies forward monitoring; they do not establish predictive power. The methodology document sets out each limitation and the evidence behind each decision.
